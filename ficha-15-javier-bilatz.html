Javier Bilatz
Argentina, Technological Artist
“NFT technology was not intended for art, but rather it was stemming from blockchain technology that rose a possible solution for a problem which concerned not art itself but the market for art in general, a problem that was exacerbated by digital art in particular; the problem with falsification and singular identification of works.”

I work on, among other topics, the naturalization of the technological conception of everyday life; incommunication, queries about subjects stemming from the geometric movements from the XX century and the tension between the figurative and the abstract.

I approach the generative stages of art as an instance for estrangement between myself and my work, when I set out to leave aside everything gestual, including manual gestures, in order to elaborate works from my choices and the reactions or ideas that arise from the consequences of those choices. I work mainly on processes and programming codes, adapting and generating formats and techniques custom made for my projects.

Duchamp said it first: “If I call it art, then it’s art”. I call, or evoke, a portion of the production of my codes and processes to be my work: bases, blueprints for physical or hybrid works made up with processes both physical and/or digital.
