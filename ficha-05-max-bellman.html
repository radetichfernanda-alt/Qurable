Max Bellman
Buenos Aires, Visual Artist
“I consider it important how having the multimedia artists’ profiles in web platforms, especially in the NFT world, allows differentiation between “bots” emulating humans and actual humans, who produce things while connected to the real world. This is a space where we can express what we feel, think, and do.”

Which is the real world?

In my childhood, I had access to a Sinclair ZX 81, a rather rudimentary computer my uncle had brought from Hong Kong, along with a Casio Piano. For a long time, that was my whole electronic world; a keyboard with four sounds, the ZX plugged into a black & white TV, and the few programmes I loaded from a cassette. These gifts were fundamental to my doings, and are still stored somewhere in my memories.

Years later, my computer and I moved from Panama to Buenos Aires, where I began studying Fine Arts in a quite traditional school where, to that moment, there was no place for the merging of art and technology. So, my group of friends and I would meet somewhere after art school to develop multimedia projects (we made videos, music, illustrations, and animations), all the while exchanging software programmes.

With these memories and experiences as a supporting structure, the idea of a possible Metaverse leads me to imagine the future becomings of spaces and bodies, to explore the different ways of bonding the real with the virtual. Call those products art (art traversed by personal and collective history, by emotions and subjectivity, even by mistakes). A load of elements are combined, which could hardly be put together by a bot.
